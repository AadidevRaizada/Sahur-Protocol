# Narrative craft brief: scary-to-ally companion, 7 cutscenes, sacrifice finale

Evidence tags: **[S]** = sourced from a fetched or searched page. **[J]** = my own design judgement, not sourced. The search tool returned thin material on Undertale/Hornet/Iron Giant first-contact staging and on Spiritfarer's rituals, so those are not claimed here.

## 1. Scary first meeting that flips to trust
1. **Keep the player unsure whether it is friend or foe.** Ueda says the boy is uncertain about Trico from the first moment, and the relationship rises and falls instead of climbing in a straight line. [S] *Sahur: the knock, then a long beat of nothing, then a second knock, softer.*
2. **Flip it with a small act of care, not a speech.** Trico's first task is pulling spears out of its body, which makes the player the rescuer. [S] *Sahur: the player sees the creature flinch at its own bat, or put the bat down, before any line is spoken.*
3. **Let the threat object change meaning.** [J] The bat is the fear object in cutscene 1. In cutscene 2 it is held handle-first or used on the thing chasing you. The same prop is the "I was terrified, then I wasn't" beat.
4. **Delay the explanation.** [J] Scare, then rescue, then a single line of warmth. Backstory comes later, in small pieces.
5. **Make a shared problem force cooperation.** The boy has no combat skills, so threats can only be beaten by working with Trico. [S] *Sahur: room 1 is a fight you cannot win alone.*
- Sources: [PlayStation Blog, Last Guardian storytelling secrets](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=176941) · [Gigazine Ueda interview](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20170115-the-last-guardian-ueda-humito-interview)

## 2. Bonding with minimal text
1. **One verb as the bond.** Ico's hand-holding is a button used only to hold hands with an NPC, and Yorda's speech is deliberately left untranslated to reinforce the language barrier. [S] *Sahur: a "knock" or "call" key the player and companion use at doors, with the companion answering.*
2. **Subtract everything that reminds players it is a game character.** Ico cut the health bar, map and looping NPC dialogue. [S] Use this for non-combat scenes: no HUD, no prompts.
3. **Call and response with a limited vocabulary.** Journey's only communication is a chime, and the restriction is what makes the bond work. [S] *Sahur: the companion's knock rhythm (3 knocks) is the recurring signal; the player can answer it.*
4. **Give the bond an asymmetric co-dependence.** The boy is strong enough to hold Trico's feathers but not self-sufficient. [S] Brothers gives each brother one stick and one trigger so the player's hands carry the relationship. [S]
5. **Escalate attachment to an object through use.** Portal testers kept forgetting the box, so the team added dialogue and heart markings, then later paid it off by incinerating it. [S] *Sahur: the bat gets scratched or wrapped with cloth over time, and the player does the wrapping.*
6. **Use varied bonding beats, not one repeated beat.** Quiet moments broaden the relationship's range. [S] Plan one comic, one tender, one shared-rest beat.
7. **A recurring line.** [J] Pick one short line (for example, the pre-dawn call) said three times: scared, shared, final. Its meaning changes each time.
- Sources: [Guinness, hand-holding record](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/457543-first-videogame-with-a-hand-holding-mechanic) · [Journey overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game)) · [Gamedeveloper, Brothers: All the Feels in One Input](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/brothers-all-the-feels-in-one-input) · [Weighted Companion Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_Companion_Cube)

## 3. Making the death land
1. **Pay off a mechanic you taught earlier.** In Brothers' final swim, the younger brother alone only wades; the player must also use the dead brother's stick/trigger to commit to the stroke. The author reads it as courage drawn from memory. [S] *Sahur: after the sacrifice, the companion's "call" key still works. Press it and nothing answers, or the player's own attack picks up the companion's rhythm.*
2. **Teach the mechanic that gets destroyed, before it is destroyed.** Portal used the cube to train the incinerator needed for the final fight, which made the cube's loss stronger. [S] *Sahur: make a companion-triggered ability (a stun, a door-knock opener) central to rooms 1-3, so its absence is felt in the boss room.*
3. **Foreshadow with mechanics, not dialogue.** [J] Flicker the companion's outline, slow its ability cooldowns, and add a cough or stagger animation across areas 2 and 3. The player feels the decline before any scene explains it.
4. **Make the empty space a design object.** Gris starts with limits (cannot jump, singing comes out as a whimper) and regains abilities as the story progresses. [S] Run it in reverse: after the death, remove the ally ability and leave the HUD slot visible but empty. [J]
5. **Role reversal before the loss.** Brothers has the younger brother support his wounded brother, inverting the swimming mechanic to show growth. [S] *Sahur: in area 3 the player protects the companion once, so the finale's sacrifice is a choice it makes in return.* [J]
6. **Callback line last.** [J] End on the recurring line, spoken by the player character or heard from off-screen.
- Sources: [Gamedeveloper, Brothers](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/brothers-all-the-feels-in-one-input) · [Steam discussion on the swim](https://steamcommunity.com/app/225080/discussions/0/540731619408058339) · [Gris, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gris) · [Gris analysis](https://amritbirdi.substack.com/p/how-the-game-gris-transforms-grief)

## 4. Flashbacks
Screenwriting guidance is consistent: [S]
1. **Answer a question the story already raised.** A flashback with no burning question to answer is dead weight.
2. **Do not just dole out information; move the present story forward.** Casablanca's Paris flashback works because the audience is already eager for it.
3. **Make the transition clean and say whose memory it is.**
4. **Use a sensory trigger.** A smell, sound or object pushes the scene into the past.
5. **Test: does it change the meaning of the present scene or what the character risks now?** Otherwise cut it.
Game-specific: object-triggered optional memories work when they carry a reward (an RPG Maker dev ties each to a new skill). [S] Playable flashbacks are the strongest form. [S]

*Sahur rules [J]:* at most one flashback (1-3 lines, under 6 seconds). It is triggered by the bat or the knock sound. It shows only the single image the present scene needs (for example, the companion at a door, in the cold, alone). It sits just before the sacrifice or right after the death, and the scene must play fine if it is skipped.
- Sources: [Script editing: flashbacks](https://scriptmag.com/screenplays/advanced-script-editing-flashbacks-voiceovers) · [NoFilmSchool flashbacks guide](https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-write-flashbacks-guide-writers-and-directors) · [TheGamer, playable flashbacks](https://www.thegamer.com/best-playable-flashback-sequences/)

## 5. Pacing story between action rooms
1. **Make each beat reactive to where the player is.** Kasavin says Hades' variability is around game state: characters talk about the exact moment you are in. [S]
2. **Keep story optional and easy to ignore.** Hades is "there if you want it and very easy to ignore." [S] *Sahur: every cutscene skippable with one key, each beat 2-8 lines.* [J]
3. **One speaker, one pass.** Hades lets you talk to a character once per return, which sets the rhythm. [S] Do one cutscene per gate, never a second.
4. **Do not let difficulty block the story.** Kasavin: struggling players should still reach the end. [S] Offer a retry-with-checkpoint or an assist toggle.
5. **Add small, unnecessary details.** Kasavin: "one of them is gonna work on you." [S] Cheap barks do this job.
6. **Let the world remember.** Bosses remember earlier fights. [S] *Sahur barks reference what just happened (a near-death, a clean room).*
7. **Vary lengths.** [J] Suggested ladder for 7 scenes: 6 lines, 3, 4, 2 (a rest), 5, 3, 8 (the end). Place the longest quiet beats right after a boss or a hard room, never before one.
8. **Barks as texture.** [J] Short lines in rooms when the companion acts, repeating a motif rather than new facts.
- Sources: [Gamedeveloper, Kasavin on roguelikes](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-with-i-hades-i-creative-director-greg-kasavin) · [GDC Vault, Breathing Life into Greek Myth](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026975/Breathing-Life-into-Greek-Myth) · [Hades loop analysis](https://itch.io/blog/774695/to-hell-and-back-again-a-look-at-loops-and-arcs-in-hades)
- Dead Cells is described as sparse, fragmentary story delivery (notes, loading-screen vignettes). [S] Not a model for a character-driven arc, only for tone. [J]

## 6. Companion HP bar in the boss fight
Escort pitfalls named in sources: [S]
- The escort dies from minimal damage, runs ahead into danger, or steps into your line of fire.
- It demands attention on top of the mission.
- Designers "lobotomize" NPC AI so it does not solve the puzzle for you.

What worked in sources: [S]
- BioShock Infinite's Elizabeth has no life bar and cannot be hurt; she is a "very you-centered AI" who helps (tosses ammo, points out enemies) rather than needing rescue. Naughty Dog said Ellie must never annoy or frustrate the player.
- Resident Evil 4's Ashley is a slightly added challenge rather than rage-inducing, because her AI is good.
- A Gangstar dev gave the companion a target priority so its death was not common.
- Caveat: invulnerability can cost immersion for some players (forum opinion).

*Sahur boss design [J], sourced-pattern plus judgement:*
1. **Downed, not dead, until the scripted sacrifice.** Companion goes to a knee at 0 HP; a hold-to-revive button gives a short window, as in coop games.
2. **Player is never the primary target.** Boss telegraphs a companion-directed attack with a clear, long wind-up; the player can body-block or interrupt it.
3. **Companion helps in meaningful ways**: a stun, a shield, or a damage window, so players want it alive.
4. **Regeneration between phases** so one mistake is not a game over.
5. **Make the finale scripted, not a fail state.** The bar drains by design in the last phase, and the sacrifice triggers when the player fills a meter or reaches a moment. Players should feel "I let it happen" and not "I failed the escort." The bar's colour or flicker matches the foreshadowing from section 3.
6. **No new rules at the finale.** Teach downed/revive in area 1 or 2, early, on a tame enemy.
- Sources: [Softpedia on Elizabeth](https://games.softpedia.com/blog/BioShock-Infinite-Diary-Elizabeth-and-Making-NPCs-Helpful-341769.shtml) · [GDC Vault, Bringing Elizabeth to Life](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020831/Bringing-BioShock-Infinite-s-Elizabeth) · [TV Tropes, Escort Mission](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EscortMission)

## Suggested 7-beat spine [J]
| # | Beat | Lines | Job |
|---|------|-------|-----|
| 1 | Night knock, bat in the doorway | 4-6 | Fear, an ambiguous threat |
| 2 | It hands you the bat / saves you from a pursuer | 3 | Flip to ally |
| 3 | Entering area 1, first shared ritual (the knock call) | 3-4 | Teach the verb |
| 4 | Rest between areas, comic beat | 2-3 | Warmth, no plot |
| 5 | Area 2 end: companion falters (mechanic foreshadow) | 4 | Dread |
| 6 | Area 3 end: you protect it; recurring line, 2nd time | 3-4 | Role reversal |
| 7 | After the sacrifice: empty slot, 1 flash memory, callback line | 6-8 | Payoff |
