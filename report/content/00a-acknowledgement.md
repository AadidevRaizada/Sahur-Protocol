We would like to express our sincere gratitude to our faculty in-charge for the Software Engineering course, whose laboratory sessions, guidance and feedback shaped every chapter of this report. The structure of the experiments, from the literature survey and requirements analysis to the use case, activity, class and sequence models, gave a playful idea the discipline of a real software project.

We thank the Department of Computer Engineering at Mukesh Patel School of Technology Management & Engineering, SVKM's NMIMS, for the curriculum, the laboratory facilities and the opportunity to carry out a mini-project of our own choosing.

We are grateful to our classmates and friends who played early builds of Sahur Protocol and told us honestly what confused them. Their reactions taught us more about usability than any checklist.

We also acknowledge the open documentation communities whose work we relied on daily, in particular the MDN Web Docs for the Canvas, Web Audio, Storage, Touch and Screen Orientation APIs, and the W3C for the accessibility guidelines that informed our design choices.

Finally, we thank our families for their patience during late nights of debugging, and for not asking too many questions about why a wooden log kept shouting "TUNG" from our laptops.

::: plain label="P.S."
If you are reading this before the rest of the report: yes, it is a game about a log with a bat fighting The AI Bubble. Yes, it is also a serious Software Engineering project. Both things can be true.
:::
